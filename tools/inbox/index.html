    How it looks in the inbox · mock-ups, widths are estimates

    Where the numbers come from. No inbox publishes how much it shows, and it changes with your device, window size, density setting and app updates, so every width here is an estimate for the screen named. Each one is the space the inbox row gives the text, worked out from the platform’s own type scale and the row’s layout:

      Fonts and sizes: Gmail uses Google Sans and Roboto at Material 3’s type scale, and its density setting changes the rows. Apple Mail uses the system font at the iOS text styles and shows 2 preview lines by default. Outlook uses Segoe UI at the Fluent 2 sizes; its preview length is becoming a setting, and 1 line is assumed. Published character counts (Gmail about 90, iPhone about 81 to 90, Outlook anywhere from 35 to 255, for example) vary that much because wide letters take more room, which is why this tool measures pixels.

      Measured on your device. The text is measured and drawn in each inbox’s font, or the closest one your device has, so a Mac and a Windows PC can differ by a word or two. Inboxes cut mid-word and add “…” where they run out of room.

      The spam checks are a guide, not a rule. Filters judge your sending history and how people react far more than any one word. ALL CAPS, piled-up “!!!” and lots of emoji mostly put readers off. When the preheader is empty, the inbox shows the first text in your email instead, which is often a “View in browser” link. When it is short, the inbox fills the rest of the line with the first words of the email.
