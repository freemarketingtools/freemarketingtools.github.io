    The cover check is an estimate. It can’t read text: it looks for sharp, high-contrast detail like letters and logos under each app’s buttons and captions. A plain background passes.

    Where the zones come from. Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels use Meta’s own figure from its safe zone guide for Stories and Reels ads: leave about 14% at the top, 35% at the bottom and 6% on each side free of text and logos. Instagram Stories uses Meta’s Stories ad specs: about 14% at the top and 20% at the bottom (the 6% sides are our estimate, borrowed from Reels).

    TikTok and YouTube Shorts are estimates. TikTok gives its safe zone as a template image on its In-Feed ads specs and says it shrinks as your caption gets longer. Google draws the Shorts zone as a picture in its Shorts ads specs and safe zone guide. Both use the commonly measured 1080×1920 values, so check the official templates before you ship.

    Format checks. 9:16 at 1080×1920 fills every screen. Wider ads are letterboxed and taller ones are cropped. TikTok’s minimum is 540×960 and its In-Feed ads run up to 10 minutes. Shorts ads run up to 3 minutes, with only the first 60 seconds in the Shorts feed. Stories plays ads under 35 seconds in full. Meta publishes no length limit for Reels.
